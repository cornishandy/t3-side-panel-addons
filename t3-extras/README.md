# T3 add-ons (t3-extras)

> **Personal experiment.** I'm not a professional developer; I use T3 Code on my Mac for personal movie and Twitter projects. I made this to suit my own workflow. It isn't an official T3 Code project or a supported product.

Small additions to **T3 Code (Nightly)** on this Mac, made 19 Oct 2026. Each one has its own switch, so you can turn off any one you don't like.

## What you get

Click **✦** in the sidebar's bottom row (next to Settings) to open the add-ons panel. Every add-on below has a switch there.

**Usage: one item per set of limits (Claude, Claude AG, Codex, Gemini, Grok).** Percentages always mean **what's left**. A dash (—) means "not known right now", never zero. Old readings are greyed out and marked *stale*. Everything is labelled **This Mac**.
- Enabled quota providers stay visible when their response contains no usage percentage. They show `— · unavailable` in the sidebar, a dash in the header, and a named unavailable section in the detail. The composer retains the tightest measured limit and adds notices such as `Grok —`. Missing readings never enter comparisons, forecasts or history. Providers turned off in T3 (even with an older reading still saved) and providers without quota support stay in the detail's separate list. When T3 has two instances of one provider, the add-ons use a turned-on one whose main window has a reading, preferring the one named like the provider.
- **Claude AG (1 Nov 2026):** Antigravity's shared "Claude & GPT" limits, which Claude (and GPT) models run on in T3's Antigravity provider (Google AI Ultra): its own item, with the weekly window as the bar and the 5-hour window as the tank. They are not your Claude plan, so they never mix with Claude's numbers, and Gemini's item keeps only Gemini's own windows. The numbers come from the same Antigravity reading T3 saves for Gemini. It shows `—` when that reading lists the shared windows without a percentage, or when T3 couldn't read Antigravity at all; an account whose reading has no shared windows gets no Claude AG item. `lab-usage` doesn't read these windows, so there's no tier or gate, and the run-out strip uses the average since the week started. In a short window the name column widens a little to fit "Claude AG".
- **The glyph ("pace needle with a 5-hour tank"):** the bar is the week (or month) remaining, like T3's own usage bars. The upright mark sits where even spending would leave you: bar past the mark means you're under pace. If the lab's reading lists a 5-hour window (Gemini and Claude AG today), the mark is a small tank, and its level is what's left of that 5-hour window, a separate limit on its own scale. The tank turns amber when the 5-hour window is spending more than 5 points faster than its own clock (T3's rule). Without a 5-hour window it's a plain line. Hover any glyph for the numbers in words.
- **Claude has no 5-hour window (13 Nov 2026):** Claude's plan dropped it, and its reading now lists the week and Fable's week only. So Claude's item has no tank and no `5h` number anywhere, a used-up week reads `0% left` with the week's refill time (nothing says "blocked"), and the detail's legend names the tank only while a lab on screen has one. Nothing is switched off: a reading that lists a 5-hour window is drawn as before, for any lab.
- **Fable's own weekly limit (13 Nov 2026):** Claude's plan meters Fable separately from the week for all models, so Claude's item carries that limit too. Nothing changes for a lab without a per-model limit.
  - **Glyph:** a thin second bar under the week's bar, in Claude's colour, in every Claude glyph (sidebar, short window, header, chip, detail). The pace needle crosses both bars when the two limits refill together.
  - **Sidebar words:** `63% left · Fable 38%`. When the line is too wide, healthy numbers give way one at a time, the less tight first, then the week's word "left". At 15% left or less Fable is always shown by name (`Fable 8% left`) with its refill time; at zero it reads `Fable out`.
  - **Your stop:** when `lab-usage` reports a numeric Fable stop (`fable_stop`), a Fable number at or below it is in the warning colour, the detail's thin bar gets a tick there, and Fable's row says "Your rules stop ordinary Fable work at 10% left". Without a stop there is no tick, and only `Fable out` is in the warning colour.
  - **Short window (one line per lab):** the week plus Fable (`80% · Fable 70%`); if that doesn't fit, Fable alone when its number is lower than the week's (`Fable 38%`), otherwise the week alone. A low Fable leads the row (`Fable 8% left · 50%`), and the week's number gives way when the row is too narrow. When the week itself is low (15% left or less), the week's number comes first, also when Fable is the tighter of the two: with a healthy Fable the row is the week and its refill clock, without Fable (`10% · Sun 3:59 PM`); with a low Fable it is the week then Fable (`3% · Fable 8% left`), on one line if they fit and two if not.
  - **With a 5-hour window too** (Claude's reading listed one until 13 Nov 2026; these rules stay for a reading that does): the tank stays on the week's bar, and the sidebar reads `63% left · Fable 38% · 5h 70%`. The short window shows the week plus the tighter of Fable and the 5-hour window (`80% · 5h 62%`). A low Fable and a low 5-hour window, with a healthy week, show tighter first (`5h 4% · Fable 8% left`), on one line if they fit and a line each if not. When the week and Fable are both low, the week's number comes first, with the 5-hour window when that is low too, then Fable (`3% · 5h 4%` over `Fable 8% left`).
  - **Equal numbers:** where two limits show the same whole percent, the broader one counts as the tighter: the week, then a 5-hour window, then Fable.
  - **Composer chip:** names Fable only when its number is strictly the lowest (`Claude Fable 38%`). A Fable limit at zero leaves that comparison, since it never blocks the other models.
  - **Detail:** a Fable row (left, used, its projection, the stop), a thin Fable line in the strip beside "All models", and Fable's own run-out sentence when it is at risk. Tooltips and spoken labels name every reading.
  - **Source:** T3's saved `seven_day_fable` window, or `lab-usage`'s per-model reading when T3 has none. A limit listed without a reading says "no reading" and draws nothing.
- **Sidebar:** one row per lab above the sidebar's bottom icons, e.g. `Gemini 63% left · 5h 70%` over its glyph (a lab with no 5-hour window has no `5h` part: `Codex 63% left`).
- **Sidebar in a short window (25 Oct 2026, on at first):** while the window is under 800 px tall, each lab takes one line instead: name, a small glyph, then `63% · 5h 70%` (plus the refill clock when one runs low). About 64 px for four labs instead of 160. Switch: ✦ → Settings → Usage → *Sidebar: compact in a short window*.
- **Header:** one small glyph per lab at the top right, no words (hover for them).
- **Composer:** the single tightest lab right now (5-hour windows count), e.g. `Gemini 5h 22%`. It hides itself when the composer is narrow.
- Click (or press Enter on) any of these for the **detail**: per lab, a run-out strip (time across the window, height = % used; dashed diagonal = even pace; solid = measured; faint dashes = estimated before logging started; dots = the projection; an amber ✕ where it would run out), with the 5-hour window as a lane below at its real time. Under it: used and left, even pace, reset times, the projection, Claude's tier and today's share, the Codex gate, challenge level and banked resets. Labs with no meter (OpenCode, Cursor, Pi, Muse) are listed by name.
- Where the numbers come from: T3's own saved quota readings (the same numbers as T3's usage page, read from T3's local storage, read only), plus `lab-usage` for Claude's tier, the Codex gate and the projections. As in T3, a window with no valid length shows no pace; the one exception is Grok, which never reports its length, so its pace assumes 7 days, and says so. `lab-usage`'s projection is used only when it matches T3's current reading (fresh, same reset, within 3 points, no drop since); otherwise the strip uses the average since the window started, or says "Estimate unavailable".

**Sidebar: Older threads section (31 Oct 2026, on at first).** Active threads last active more than 2 days ago fold under one header, "Older than 2 days", drawn like T3's own Settled shelf header: the label on the left, the count and a chevron on the right. Click it to show or fold them; it stays put and remembers its state. Pick the age under ✦ → Settings → Sidebar → *Older than*: 12 hours, 1 day, 2 days (the default), 3 days, 1 week, or Auto (the earlier rule: threads idle well past your newest few, and only when 2 or more would fold). Ages are T3's own, rounded down, so "2d" is 48 hours or more. The open thread, a row being renamed, and rows showing a status (Working, Done, Approval…) never fold. Any Shift, Cmd or Ctrl selection shows every older thread until a plain click or Escape, since T3 selects across hidden rows.

**Replies: Footer team list.** How the team list in the reply footer behaves: *Always show*, *Start open, click to fold* (the default), *Start folded, click to open*, or *Always hide*. "Start folded" folds each footer's list once, when it first appears, by clicking its own toggle; after that it's yours to open. The look for each choice belongs to the reply footer card below.

**Replies: Footer Made so far list.** How the Made so far list in the reply footer behaves: *Start folded*, *Open short lists* (the default), *Always open*, or *Hide*. "Open short lists" opens a list of five or fewer on the newest reply when the chat pane is wide; a narrower pane and older replies stay folded. A click on a link in a list the add-on opened by itself holds that list open while its reply is the newest, even if the pane narrows; it folds like any older reply once a newer reply arrives. A list you open or close yourself stays put. "Always open" keeps every list open. "Hide" hides the list. The look for each choice belongs to the reply footer card below.

**Thread burn tells.** When a thread is spending fast, its sidebar row shows it (switch: ✦ → Settings → Usage → *Thread burn tells*, on at first). Dollars are an estimated API cost equivalent, priced the way T3's usage page prices them; not your subscription charge.
- **Short burst:** a small amber dot by the row's time, with a tooltip ("Short burst: about $1.90 in the last 2 minutes"). It fades 5 minutes after the burst ends.
- **Running hot (building):** a burst still going after 8 minutes: the dot plus a thin strip.
- **Sustained heat:** a 3-pixel strip on the row's right edge and a chip like `🔥 $31/h · 42 min`: amber (warm), orange (hot), red with a slow pulse and a faint tint (blazing). It needs ten minutes of continuous spending, so one big spike followed by silence never counts. The open thread's header shows the same chip.
- The lines come from your own last 7 days ($46/h for a burst, $18, $32 and $41/h for warm, hot and blazing), recomputed every 6 hours and never lower than those. The tooltip compares with your usual rate for that model.
- Antigravity threads (Gemini, and Claude AG's Claude models) aren't measured (their usage isn't in a readable form).
- When a usage limit runs low (a 5-hour window at 25% or less, or ahead of its clock, or a week at 15% or less), its refill time joins the sidebar label, the glyph's tooltip and the composer chip, e.g. `Gemini 58% left · 5h 12% · refills 1:59 AM`. Every refill in the details shows the clock time too.

**Handover.**
- **Prepare handover** (in the composer): adds a ready-made "hand this thread over to a fresh thread" request **to the end of** what you've typed. It never replaces your text and never sends it: you read it and press Enter yourself. Undo (Cmd-Z) takes it out again. If it can't be added safely, it offers **Copy handover request** instead. It's greyed out on drafts and on threads that run on another machine.
- **Handover chain pill** (top right): on a thread with a living handoff file it shows, e.g., **Handover 2** (the second thread in its chain). Click it for the chain's history and a **Copy file path** button.

**Sidebar.**
- **Compact threads (25 Oct 2026, on at first):** each thread is one line: the project icon, then the title (with one-row titles off, two or three lines at most, or No limit to show the full title, as picked under that switch; used only as needed), then the status. The project name and the branch line (branch or machine, diff counts, model icons) are hidden; hover the project icon for a small tooltip with the project name and the branch or machine. Hover actions (Settle, snooze) sit over the end of the title, so a row never re-wraps under the pointer. A short rule, a quarter of the row wide and lined up with the icons, separates neighbouring threads. While it's on, the three switches below don't apply and are hidden. CSS grid on T3's own row plus one tooltip listener; T3's nodes are not moved.
- **Tighter rows:** less space between threads.
- **Model icon beside the title:** on a thread whose bottom line held only the model icon, the icon moves next to the title and the empty line goes away. Rows that also show a branch or PR stay as they were.
- **Stronger Working divider:** the **Working** header above running threads takes T3's own working colour, in bold, with a thicker line and a faint band, instead of T3's faint grey.
- **Selected thread marker:** a 3-pixel bar down the left edge plus a bold title, so the open thread stands out even in themes with faint highlights. You can pick **Outline** or **Off** instead.

**Fonts.** The compact Add-ons menu opens one dedicated Fonts workspace. A searchable index groups all 18 areas by Your text, Assistant replies and App & interface, showing each resolved font, size and source. Wide windows show the index and one persistent editor; narrow windows use a list and editor with Back. Scope descriptions and existing target switches precede font controls. Each area can use the current T3 default, its own font, or follow another area’s family and size. Edit source has a separate Back-to-follower path. Own choices remain saved in the other modes; links that would create a cycle are unavailable. Changes apply immediately. The existing 51-family catalog, filters, Previous/Next, name previews and 80–150% size controls remain. Composer and Quotes inherit the former Body choice once; raw sent-prompts choices and schema 2 remain independent. The old Interface plan-text setting migrates to a Plans source link when appropriate, including a persisted Off pick, while an explicit source wins.
- **T3's own fonts (25 Oct 2026).** Every font menu starts with a **T3 Code settings** group: *T3 interface font*, *T3 composer font* and *T3 code font* follow whatever T3's Settings → Appearance currently uses (its `--font-sans`, `--font-composer` and `--font-mono`), and *T3 system font* is T3's built-in default. No font file is loaded: the area's CSS falls back to that variable (`--t3x-base-<area>` in the owned `#t3x-dyn` style), so the size buttons don't apply. T3's terminal font isn't offered: T3 gives it to the terminal directly, not to the page. Visualizations don't list these (their sandboxed frames can't see T3's variables).
- **Your fonts (24 Oct 2026).** The ✦ panel lists each font you picked under Fonts…: the area, any areas that follow it, its place in the list, and the name written in that font, with ‹ › to try the previous or next font (← → work too when the name has focus). Clicking a name opens Fonts on that area. It only appears once an area has its own font. **Size (7 Nov 2026):** − and + on each font's label line change its size in 5% steps, 80–150%: the same setting as Size in Fonts, so areas that follow it change too. T3's own fonts don't show them, since they take T3's size; Default in Fonts puts a font back to its own size.
- **Use for similar (26 Oct 2026).** Optional, never automatic. An area with its own font offers "Use for similar" (on its ✦ panel row, and under Font source in Fonts) when close-enough areas don't use that font yet; one click makes them follow it, and Undo puts each back as it was. The groups: Replies, Quotes, Reasoning, Plans and Questions; Message composer and Your prompts; Headings and Thread titles; Tables and Reply footer; Links and File paths; Sidebar labels, Tooltips, Settings text and Usage page. Visualizations has no group. An area that had its own font keeps it stored while it follows, and the button's tooltip names it before you click.
- **Find fonts on screen (25 Oct 2026).** For "which font option controls this bit of the screen?". **Pick a font from the screen** (✦ panel, under Fonts…) or **Pick from screen** (Fonts window header) starts it; the bar reads "Reading this screen's fonts…" for a moment, then:
  - **Hover** any text: an outline and a tag name the area that controls it, its current font, and "click to change". Digits say they keep T3's font (font overrides never include 0-9); code, controls and foreign content say "Not one of the Fonts areas".
  - **Hold Shift:** every area on screen gets a dashed outline and a label (up to 40; labels that would overlap are skipped). Clicking a label is the same as clicking that text.
  - **Click** the text: Fonts opens *docked* — the same dialog, non-modal and compact, beside the paragraph so the change shows live, with the font list showing straight away. **Pick other text**, **All fonts…** (the full window on that area) and **Done** sit at its foot. If the text sits under one of the area's Applies to boxes that is off, the card says so.
  - **Show on screen** (in the Fonts editor, docked or full) outlines every place on screen that area applies to, or would once it has a font; the full window steps aside until a click or Esc.
  - Esc stops; clicks while picking never reach T3. Embedded visualization frames get an owned cover while picking, so they can be picked too (they belong to Visualizations).
  - Switches under Settings → **Fonts on screen**: *Pick from screen*, *Change it beside the text* (off: a click opens the full window), *Shift labels everything*, *Show on screen*.
  - How it knows: the live CSS decides. The picker briefly switches the add-on's own `<html>` font attributes (`data-t3x-f*`, `data-t3x-ft-*`) and reads `getComputedStyle`, restoring them in the same task. An area is named only when switching **that one area** on (the others as they are) gives the text its face, so it can't name a control that wouldn't change the text. Transitions are off (`html[data-t3x-probing]`) for the whole session. Answers are cached per screen and cleared whenever a font switches on or off; a click re-reads its text.
- **Move popups out of the way (7 Nov 2026).** For "the popup covers the very text whose font I'm changing". Every popup's top bar has a ⠿ grip: the ✦ panel, the usage, handover and chain popups, the Fonts window, the docked Fonts card and the Find-fonts bar.
  - **Drag** the bar (or ⠿) anywhere. The popup reopens there, even after T3 restarts; its spot is measured from the window edges nearest it, so one dragged to the right stays on the right when the window changes size. Arrow keys on ⠿ move it 16 px (Shift: 64); **Home** on ⠿, or a double-click on the bar, puts it back. The ✦ panel's top bar stays in reach while its settings scroll under it.
  - **Fonts** no longer dims the screen. The full window can hang off an edge (160 px of its header always stays in) and has a **Small card** button: it becomes the docked card for the area being edited, placed where it covers none of that area's text on screen (or the least of it), beside that text. A card opened by picking text goes clear of the area's text the same way, and keeps clear of the text just picked whenever there's room. A card you drag reopens at that spot, unless it would cover the text just picked. The card's Pick other text, All fonts… and Done stay in view while the rest scrolls. **All fonts…** goes back to the full window at its own spot. The full window stays modal; the card is the way to keep scrolling the chat.
  - Switch: Settings → **Popups** → *Move popups out of the way*. Off: the old placement and dim, no ⠿, no Small card. Spots live in the add-on's settings (`popSpots`); *Reset defaults* clears them.
- Heading applies to the existing project-name span, assistant reply headings h1 through h6, and add-on-pill targets. Headings stay off inside quotes, plans, reasoning, questions and the reply footer, even when those areas are on T3's font. Grouping group-toggle labels are not headings. Replies applies to assistant reply paragraphs/lists. Your prompts applies to messages you have sent, independently of Replies and Message composer. On first load it copies the former Main family and size if Your messages was enabled (including its default); otherwise it starts off. An explicit saved choice, including Off, always wins. The stored legacy switch is retained only for migration compatibility. Composer and Quotes have their own picker and target switch.
- Reasoning applies to expanded thinking prose; collapsed work rows and tool output keep T3's fonts. Questions applies to open question prose (the question text in the card above the composer) its option labels and descriptions, and saved answers, with the card header, radio dots and number shortcuts unchanged. Reasoning and Questions name T3's own markup as well as the native bridge's marks, so they keep working after a T3 update removes the bridge (checked against build 3707; a test copies that markup). Plans applies to proposed-plan prose, including its headings. The Interface Proposed plan text switch no longer paints plans. If that switch was already on, Plans had no explicit source and Interface had its own face, Plans follows Interface; this preserves the legacy appearance even with a stored Plans Off pick.
- Settings text has its own font and preserves the original target switches: settings headings/labels/descriptions, menu group headings, popover titles, dialog/alert/side-sheet titles and descriptions, notification titles and descriptions, and add-on headings/notes. The obsolete proposed-plan switch is retained in stored data for migration; Plans has its own area. Target switches stay saved while the source is default or linked. Buttons and control labels stay on T3's fonts. Thread titles, tooltips, the usage page and sidebar status have their own workspace editors. Extra settings notes, theme headings, account pages and the diagnostics table are not covered.
- Visualization applies to compatible native HTML and Pele diagrams when the separately checked native bridge is active in the running T3 session. The frame stays opaque and sandboxed. Native HTML saved before installation is upgraded when served; future HTML gets the same loader. Themes and authored font declarations remain independent. Diagram font changes invalidate measured text widths and recompute layout. Expanded diagrams recompute geometry too: bundled fonts embed their matching face, while Mac fonts or missing metadata use a fresh layout in T3's own font. Authored styles, images, canvas and app cards can still use their own fonts.
- Font overrides exclude digits 0-9 only. Code, diffs, the terminal and control labels keep T3's fonts. Links, marked paths, ordinary tables, the reply footer, thread titles, tooltips, usage-page text and sidebar status follow their own saved choices, and use the current T3 default until customized or linked. Names/paths supplied as unmarked description prose follow that description's selected font. Font-name previews use full faces and remain separate from applied text faces.
- Coverage boundaries: settings rows’ extra div notes, theme-search and color-scheme headings, account-page prose, diagnostics tables and unverified slim/draft sidebar variants stay native. Native menu and command items, inputs, shortcuts, tool output, code, diffs and terminal text stay native. Use T3’s interface/composer/technical typography settings for these. Foreign MCP app interiors, authored fonts, images, math and canvas are outside these overrides. Usage is marked only on its verified route/container, never by a global heading/table rule. Sidebar metadata targets informational native leaves and grouping leaf classes; it does not paint grouping controls, icons, titles or project headings. This is a category disposition, not a claim to customize every string in T3.
- The native bridge is a version-checked local archive patch, separate from the sidebar keeper. Its audited source, manifests, full archive backup, tests and rollback command remain in `~/Library/Caches/t3-font-sections/`. A T3 update can replace the patch; reapplication requires a new checked plan against that build. It does not change keeper settings, credentials, trust or global app settings, and never quits T3 automatically.


**Reply footer card.** The footer every agent ends its replies with (`---`, a plan table, a Made so far list once the thread has made things to open, a team list when other agents worked, a small usage line; rules in `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`, "End every reply to Andy with a footer") shows as one card: the verdict red or green in a tinted header with progress and ETA on the right, finished steps crossed out, running steps in the theme's accent with a pulsing dot, open items tinted, with only that row's action word underlined, who and when on the right, table controls hidden. The Made so far list and the team list under it are small and plain. It's plain CSS at the end of `public/extras.css`, and applies only to the three-column table right after a reply's last horizontal rule when it carries the verdict marker, and to the Made so far list, the team list and the small line after it. The team list follows `html[data-t3x-footer-team]` (`always-show`, `start-open`, `start-folded`, `always-hide`), set by the ✦ panel's "Footer team list" switch. The Made so far list is the collapsible right after the card whose summary carries `itemprop="made"`; it follows `html[data-t3x-footer-made]` (`start-folded`, `open-short`, `always-open`, `hide`), set by the *Footer Made so far list* switch, and its "Mac only" words (`itemprop="mac-only"`) are hidden once the add-on has confirmed the Mac app (`html[data-t3x-mac-app]`). T3 draws a reply's `<details>` as its own collapsible, so the CSS targets `[data-markdown-details]`. Round 9's two-column footers in older replies keep T3's own table look. Without the add-ons the footer shows as T3's own table and collapsible (on a phone, scroll the table sideways). Its browser test is `~/Library/Caches/footer-redesign/draft/test/run.sh` (T3's own CSS, the same Markdown pipeline and T3's collapsible markup).

## Switching things off

- **One add-on:** open **✦**, open **Settings** (the switches start folded away under Fonts), and flip its switch. Your choices are saved in T3 (`t3x:settings:v1`). **Reset defaults** puts them all back.
- **All add-ons, keeping the service:** `node ~/.local/share/t3-sidebar-keeper/keeper.mjs --disable extras`, then press Shift-Cmd-R in T3. This takes the small loader out of T3 and leaves the sidebar keeper's other fixes alone. `--enable extras` brings it back.
- **Remove everything:** `./install.sh --uninstall` in this folder (stops and deletes the service), then the `--disable extras` line above. If the service is stopped, T3 simply shows nothing extra.

## How it works (for whoever maintains it)

- **Service:** `server.mjs` (`#!/usr/bin/env node`) runs all the time as the LaunchAgent `com.cornishandy.t3-extras`. The installer puts the Node it found on the agent's `PATH`. It listens on **127.0.0.1:8793 only**: never the network, never the tailnet. It refuses other Host names (a browser DNS-rebinding guard, not an account check) and answers CORS only for T3's own origin, `t3code://app` (the same kind of browser check). It has no write endpoints, apart from the opt-in debug log (below). See [../SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md).
  - `/state` includes `tools: { labUsage, handover }`. `lab-usage` is `T3X_LAB_USAGE` or `~/.claude/bin/lab-usage`. When that file is executable, `/state` uses `lab-usage --json --read-only` (`--read-only` was added for this: no lock, no reset, nothing saved), at most every 3 minutes while T3 is open, plus on demand when a popover opens (at most every 30 seconds, never two at once). When it is missing, the service does not spawn it, does not report an error, drops any cached view, and the panel keeps T3's own readings. Tier, gate and the lab-usage projection stay hidden. The same check runs on the 15-second refresh.
  - `/history` returns the service's usage samples (bounded), and `/font-catalog` the pickers' font list (names, verdicts, measurements; no URLs). Like everything else, only to T3's own page.
  - `/state` also reads the thread's chain from `~/.local/share/handover/state.json`, read only, including the handoff file path. The handover tool is `T3X_HANDOVER_BIN` or `~/.claude/bin/handover`. When that file is not executable, the Prepare handover button and the chain pill stay hidden, and the ✦ switches say "needs the handover tool". The insert action does not run. The chain is still in `/state` when the state file exists, so Copy file path works again as soon as the tool is back. A thread ID with or without the `mcp:` prefix counts as the same thread. The poll keeps running either way.
  - `/state` also gives this Mac's T3 environment ID (`~/.t3/userdata/environment-id`).
- **Loader:** T3's page refuses outside scripts, so the sidebar keeper's unit `extras` puts a tiny inline loader (`t3x-boot-2`) into T3's `index.html`, re-applied after every nightly update. The loader fetches `/version`, `/extras.css`, `/extras.js` and `/extras.sig`, checks an ECDSA P-256 signature over `JSON.stringify(["t3x1", version, css, js])`, and inserts the style and the script only when the check passes. The public key is the one in `loader-key.json` at patch time. The private key never leaves the installer. A fake listener can replay an older pair this install really signed. It cannot insert anything else. If `grouping.js` or `grouping.css` is put back beside the served files, the service appends them and the signature no longer matches, so nothing is inserted. When you switch back to T3's window it checks `/version` and reloads the add-ons if they changed.
- **Usage numbers:** the add-ons read T3's own saved quota readings in T3's window (its IndexedDB `t3code:connection-runtime`, store `server-config`): only if that database already exists, with no version (so it can never be created or upgraded), in one read-only transaction, closing it at once. Only numbers, labels, times and status are kept; nothing about accounts. An unexpected shape shows "—" rather than a guess.
- **Burn tells:** every 30 s while T3 is open, the service asks T3's own database (`statev2.sqlite`, opened read-only, one query in a worker that is stopped after 2 s, closed straight away) which threads changed in the last 40 minutes and which provider session each one is. It then reads only the new lines of those sessions' transcripts (Claude under `~/.claude/projects`, Codex under `~/.codex/sessions` and `~/.codex-t3-api/sessions`, Grok under `~/.grok/sessions`; each file checked to be the right session inside the provider's own folder), with caps on files, bytes and time per tick. A whole record is read into memory and only the usage fields are kept. A missing directory is no data, not an error. A charge or transcript that two threads both claim is left out rather than counted twice. `/burn` answers with tiers and rates per thread id only: no titles, text or paths.
- **Usage history** (for the run-out strip): the window logs one sample per lab per 10 minutes into its own small database (`t3x-usage-history`, never T3's), and the service logs `lab-usage` samples to `~/.local/share/t3-extras/usage-history.jsonl`. Both keep 15 days and have hard size caps; the file is compacted through a temp file and an atomic rename. If either can't be written, the strip just shows less.
- **Fonts:** `install.sh` downloads the 46 Google families once (latin, one weight), from the pinned fonts.gstatic.com URLs in `fonts.json`, each with its own licence (SIL Open Font Licence, or Apache 2.0 for five of them) from github.com/google/fonts at one pinned commit. Each file must match its sha256. They're served only from that list, and T3 fetches chosen families plus lazy name previews: selected labels when Fonts opens, and the visible list on dropdown interaction, at most four preview jobs at once. Names use independent faces; role application never waits on their queue. Older native menus may keep the system font. The 5 macOS families need nothing. `fonts.json` also holds each family's style verdict and measurements; `tools/fonts.mjs` rebuilds it.
- **Safety in T3:** the add-ons only add their own elements (marked `data-t3x`) and CSS. If something they need isn't on screen, they skip that add-on, and the panel says so. They pause themselves after repeated errors rather than slow T3 down.
- **Debug log:** off by default. Setting `localStorage["t3x:diag"] = "1"` in T3 makes the add-ons send counts and on/off flags (never titles or message text) to `~/.local/share/t3-extras/diag.log`.

`node --test test/` checks: the host guard and CORS; that only loopback answers; the fonts allowlist and the font list; one shared usage snapshot; at most one lab-usage run at a time with the 30-second floor; the history file's spacing, caps and atomic compaction; reading T3's saved readings (fail closed, nothing private kept); T3's pace maths; the glyph and strip geometry; the projection and its edge cases; bare and `mcp:` thread IDs; the stale and unknown rules; route parsing; and settings, including the 3A–3D migration and independent, initially off interface-font targets. ## Sidebar groups

**Not installed since 25 Oct 2026** (Andy uses only T3's native list): `install.sh` no longer copies `public/grouping.js` and `public/grouping.css` and removes installed copies, so the service stops appending them and the Group / Sort / Native bar is gone. The source, its tests and the `/grouping` route stay; installing those two files again brings it back. What follows describes it as built.

A control at the top of the left thread list groups this Mac's top-level threads. The list is built as navigation: Group, Sort and Native stay on screen, with the scope and the returned count under them. An unfinished read stays visible in one short line, and that line does not treat every State unavailable row as only a cutoff. The method, the cap, omitted rows and the snapshot time sit in one Index details disclosure. Each row has a wrapping title, then the project and the age, then the state on its own line (the words State unavailable stay exact). Known badges stay separate from that state. A selected row uses the sidebar's selected surface and a narrow edge. Keyboard focus is its own outline. Group headers use an outline icon, a chevron and a count. A collapsed header says when it is hiding a recorded request, child work, a run, or State unavailable. **Native shelves** (the default) leaves T3's own list in charge. **Activity** groups by recorded work: Questions & approvals, Run in progress, Child work recorded, Other open threads, or State unavailable. It never says Ready, Idle or Finished. **Recent run activity** groups by the latest request, start or finish, not a token update: last 15 minutes, 15–60 minutes, earlier today, yesterday, the previous 7 days, and older. A row marked created has no run event, and only after both the run-time read and the request-time read finished. Unread timing stays Timing unavailable, not created or Older. An observed time is shown as observed, not as the verified latest event. **Project** groups by each thread's project id, labelled Project (local paths). That id is the physical project, not T3's repository grouping. Group and Sort show Recent runs for that choice. The accessible name stays Recent run activity, meaning request, start and finish times, not a token update. Sort (Recent runs, title, or created) applies only to this index. Search, or a selected project filter, hands the list back to T3. The index does not move, snooze, settle or reorder T3's rows. Snoozed and Settled start collapsed, with their counts. Menus, drag and multiselect stay on Native shelves.

Child work is recorded at most two levels in this snapshot, not the Waiting shelf, and there is no pull-request wait. If child evidence, or recorded requests, approvals or plans, are cut off, those threads say State unavailable instead of showing no blocker. A known approval or recorded child stays on the row when a different fact is still unread. Every empty Questions line is about this group. The short line is “No questions or approvals in this group.” The longer line stays when a known request sits on another row, including a pinned row, or when a request read is still unfinished. A finished read of a pending input whose response type is missing or not one this index knows stays State unavailable, with no question badge, and does not turn that line into a claim that the snapshot has no questions. A read that is still going at 2 seconds returns the threads already listed. A thread whose rows were not all read says State unavailable. An empty thread, or one whose rows were all read, can still show that no blocker was recorded.

`/grouping` reads this Mac's top-level v2 threads (not subagents; forks stay) from `statev2.sqlite`, opened read-only in its own worker and stopped after 2 seconds, returning the latest safe checkpoint instead of dropping the list. That checkpoint is published after each completed fact query (latest run, live run, limit run, pending request, plan, and child) before the next query, and again after the live approval seed and each finished history chunk, before the next statement. A live run is not called absent until both the latest-run and live-run reads have finished. A test delay only waits. The answer is metadata only: titles, project ids, recorded run and request states, and coverage counts. No message text, paths, prompts or secrets. The same host and origin rules as the other routes apply. The index stops at 1,000 threads and says when the list or the supporting facts are cut short. When `grouping.js` and `grouping.css` are installed, they are served on the end of `extras.js` and `extras.css`.

The files are `grouping.mjs`, `grouping-core.mjs`, `grouping-sql-worker.mjs`, `public/grouping.js` and `public/grouping.css`. `node --test test/` includes this index's activity rules, its read-only snapshot caps and origin guard, and a headless browser check of the grouped list.
